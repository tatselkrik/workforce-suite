# Approval delegation review —0.1.48

Open **Leave > Approval delegation**. The current increment covers leave,
attendance-correction and overtime review. Financial approvals remain separate.

- As Administrator, select an original approver and distinct eligible substitute,
  choose one workflow, dates and a reason, and save. Confirm it appears in history.
- Try the same person twice, past dates, too long a period, an overlapping grant
  and an HR/Admin approver with a lower-role substitute. Each must be rejected.
- Sign in as the assigned substitute. Pending assigned requests should appear in
  the normal workflow review list, with an explanation of whom they cover.
  The delegation should not expose foreign personnel, contacts or payroll data.
- Approve/reject a leave request and inspect its original plan and decision
  history. The original assignment remains; the actual actor and whom they
  covered appear in retained receipts. Self-review must stay blocked.
- Review an attendance correction or overtime proposal. Source facts must still
  match. A correction changes attendance only when approved; overtime keeps its
  saved approval minutes and existing payroll protections.
- Revoke a grant as Administrator. Refresh the substitute's session: unacted
  assigned work disappears, further delegated review is denied, and previous
  decision receipts remain. Future grants cannot act early; expired grants stop.
- Change/disable one participant's staff access. The grant must stop operating.
  Restoring the same binding/role within its dates can restore eligibility;
  revocation is the permanent stop for that particular grant.
- Check light/dark layouts, compact display and independent expansion of each
  **HR/Admin record** panel. Managers should see no configuration form or private
  reasons/references.

The automated verification separately covers actor-bound identical retries,
failed-refresh recovery, forged metadata, current reporting, no forwarding,
private table denial and legacy leave-read compatibility. Do not enter real
payroll or attendance changes solely to reproduce a test; use fictional records.
